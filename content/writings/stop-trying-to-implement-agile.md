---
title: Stop Trying to Implement Agile
date: 2026-11-25
tags: []
---

“You are an honest man.” What does that mean?

Can you be half honest? Can you consider yourself an honest person while deliberately choosing to be dishonest once in a while?

Honesty is not something you implement. Nor is it something you achieve by following a predefined set of practices. It is a quality reflected in your actions and decisions. You either embody it or you don’t.

The same applies to being agile.

It's called the Manifesto for Agile Software Development, not the Agile Manifesto. The manifesto itself isn't agile. It won't start running around by itself.

Agile is not a noun. It's an adjective. You don't “do agile.” You don't implement agile. You are agile, or you aren't.

Some methodologies such as Scrum is seen as a path to becoming agile. But following Scrum doesn't automatically make you agile, just as donating money to a charity institution doesn't automatically make you a good person.

Consider SAFe. It packages agility into something that can be sold to companies that don't know what they're doing.

It also gives executives something to hide behind. Promise investors better metrics, tell them the implementation will take a year or two, some nonsense random numbers tell them that progress is being made, and postpone the decisions that actually matter.

Now, look at the very first value of the manifesto:

“Individuals and interactions over processes and tools.”

What is the first thing many companies do when they decide to become agile? They hire people from outside the team to introduce processes, tools and control mechanisms.

The reason is simple. They're afraid of being wrong. Cowardly and mediocre people outsource their decisions.

Metrics offer another place to hide. Throughput, lead time and story points don't tell you whether you've delivered what was most valuable, what return it generated or how much the software actually cost.

Being agile means being an adult. Autonomy comes with the responsibility of your actions, decisions, and its outcomes. It means taking risks, making decisions and accepting their consequences instead of hiding behind a process.

Like honesty, agility is not something you implement. It is something you demonstrate through your actions and decisions.
